Predictive Maintenance

NASA C-MAPSS · Time-Series Regression

The Problem: Turbofan engines degrade gradually until sudden failure. Airlines and defense operators face unplanned downtime costing $50K–$500K per event. Manual inspection cycles are expensive and miss early warning signs.

What We Built: A rolling-window XGBoost model that processes 21 sensor channels every cycle to predict Remaining Useful Life (RUL). Feature engineering extracts rate-of-change and rolling-mean signals that raw sensor readings obscure.

Key Finding: Only 5 sensors (EGT, fan speed, core speed, LPC temp, HPC temp) capture 90% of predictive signal. A 5-sensor IoT deployment costs 75% less than full 21-sensor instrumentation while maintaining 92% of model performance.

Why This Matters: You get 25+ cycle advance warning of engine failure. That means scheduled maintenance instead of emergency grounding. For a fleet of 100 engines, that's $2M–$5M in avoided downtime annually.

Demand Forecasting

UCI Bike Sharing · Time-Series Forecasting

The Problem: Bike-sharing fleets struggle with rebalancing — too many bikes at docks during low demand, shortages during commuter peaks. Operators waste truck rolls moving inventory that could have been predicted.

What We Built: An ensemble model combining SARIMA for seasonal baselines with XGBoost for weather and calendar effects. Captures commuter peaks (8 AM, 5–6 PM), weekend leisure patterns, and temperature sensitivity.

Key Finding: Calendar patterns (hour of day, day of week, month) explain 73% of demand variance. Weather adds 18% more signal. The ensemble outperforms either model alone by 18% MAE reduction.

Why This Matters: Predictable low-demand windows let you reduce overstocking by 22% while maintaining 98% peak availability. For a 500-dock system, that's 40 fewer daily truck rolls and $300K+ in annual operational savings.

NLP Text Classification

20 Newsgroups · Multi-Class NLP

The Problem: Enterprises process thousands of unstructured documents daily — support tickets, emails, feedback forms. Manual routing is slow, inconsistent, and doesn't scale. Deep learning models are accurate but require GPU infrastructure most teams don't have.

What We Built: A production NLP pipeline using TF-IDF vectorization with Naive Bayes, Logistic Regression, and Linear SVM. Tested on 18,846 real Usenet posts across 20 distinct topics with full preprocessing (tokenization, stopword removal, lemmatization).

Key Finding: Naive Bayes with TF-IDF scores 68% on 20 categories and runs 400× faster than BERT inference with only a 21% accuracy trade-off. Primary confusion: sci.electronics vs sci.crypt (shared technical jargon).

Why This Matters: You get production-grade text classification on CPU infrastructure. No GPU costs, no API latency, no vendor lock-in. Route support tickets, classify legal documents, or triage medical notes — all on commodity hardware.
